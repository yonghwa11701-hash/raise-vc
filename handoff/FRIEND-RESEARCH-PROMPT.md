# Research prompt for a teammate (Claude Desktop, no coding)

## How to use (2 minutes)
1. Open **Claude Desktop** → start a **new chat**.
2. Turn on **web search** (the tools/search toggle under the message box). If you see **Research**, turn that on too.
3. Copy everything inside the box below, replace the two `[...]` lines, and send.
4. When Claude finishes, copy its answer and send it to Yong Hwa (WhatsApp or AirDrop). Part 6 is a prompt that goes straight into Lovable.
5. If it stops halfway, type: `continue`.

---

```
You are my research partner at a 4-hour student hackathon (Lovable Buildathon, Imperial College London, tonight 18:00–22:00). Teams build a working web-app MVP with Lovable (an AI app builder) and pitch to judges at the end. My teammate builds the app; my job is the research. I don't code, so give me plain English.

Our idea: [ONE OR TWO SENTENCES ON THE IDEA]
Who it's for: [WHO HAS THE PROBLEM, e.g. "UK university students"]

Search the web and give me the following. Be quick and concrete. Keep the whole answer under about 900 words.

1. THE PROBLEM IN ONE LINE + PROOF: 3 facts or numbers that show the problem is real and common. Give each a source link and the year. Prefer UK data, then global. If you can't find a number, say "not found". Never make one up.

2. WHO EXACTLY: the single best first user group (be narrow). Describe one realistic person in that group in 2 sentences.

3. WHAT EXISTS ALREADY: up to 5 competitors or workarounds (apps, websites, spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups). For each: name, link, what it does, price if shown, and the gap we can fill. Then one sentence: why ours is different.

4. MARKET SIZE (rough): a simple bottom-up estimate (number of people × how much they'd pay per year). Show the sum and say which numbers are guesses.

5. HOW IT MAKES MONEY: the 2 most realistic business models, with one example company for each.

6. THE MVP TO BUILD TONIGHT: the 3 screens or features that show the core idea in under 4 hours. Leave out everything else (no logins unless essential, no payments). Then write ONE ready-to-paste prompt for Lovable that describes this app: the pages, the buttons, the data it stores, and a clean modern look. Use realistic sample data, not real people's data.

7. PITCH (60 seconds): a hook line, problem, solution, demo moment, why us, the ask. Then the 3 hardest questions a judge could ask, each with a short honest answer.

8. RISKS: anything about privacy, health or safety claims, or legal limits we should avoid saying in the pitch.

Rules:
- Put a source link after every fact. Mark anything you're inferring as (estimate).
- If our idea is weak, say so plainly and suggest one sharper version of it.
- Use headings and short bullet points so I can read it on my phone.
```
